Truth is a matter of the imagination. The soundest fact may fail or prevail in the style of its telling: like that singular organic jewel of our seas, which grows brighter as one woman wears it and, worn by another, dulls and goes to dust. Facts are no more solid, coherent, round and real than pearls are. But both are sensitive.

— Ursula K. Le Guin The Left Hand of Darkness, Gently Ai

On the fickle nature of "Truth".

Fiction 6 September 2026 §

The universe is change; our life is what our thoughts make it.

— Marcus Aurelius Meditations, Book IV, 3

The second clause is the one that survives contact with a bad week. The first is the premise it rests on — if nothing holds still, then the only fixed point available is the stance you take, not the thing you take it towards.

Fiction 20 August 2026 §

There is grandeur in this view of life, with its several powers, having been originally breathed into a few forms or into one; and that, whilst this planet has gone cycling on according to the fixed law of gravity, from so simple a beginning endless forms most beautiful and most wonderful have been, and are being, evolved.

— Charles Darwin On the Origin of Species, closing paragraph

The one sentence in the book where he lets himself write like this. Worth noticing that “the fixed law of gravity” is the anchor he reaches for when he wants to sound certain — physics as the thing biology borrows authority from.
